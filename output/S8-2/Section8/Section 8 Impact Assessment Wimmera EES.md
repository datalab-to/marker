# 8 MITIGATION MEASURES

Iluka has a general environmental duty (GED) under the *Environmental Protection Act 2017* (EP Act) to eliminate or minimise the risk of harm to human health and the environment—including biodiversity and ecological values—arising from construction or operational activities, pollution, and waste.

The project will be conducted in accordance with an approved work plan issued under the *Mineral Resources (Sustainable Development) Act 1990* (MRSD Act), and in line with the Environmental Management Framework (EMF) for the project. Both the work plan and EMF require that detailed design minimises or avoids impacts to native vegetation and habitat to the extent practicable within the Disturbance Footprint.

The primary biodiversity impacts are expected to occur due to land clearance during the establishment of the mine layout and construction of ancillary infrastructure (e.g., powerlines and water pipelines).

The focus of mitigation measures is to:  
1. **Avoid** impacts where possible;  
2. **Minimise** residual impacts through project-specific measures; and  
3. **Progressively rehabilitate** land in consultation with local stakeholders to support productive agriculture consistent with surrounding areas.

Key mitigation and management measures (Table 8) are outlined below. These measures are based on best practice and proven effectiveness in managing ecological impacts.

## 8.1 Mitigation Measures

### 8.1.1 Awareness of Ecological Values

#### 8.1.1.1 Inductions (EMo1)

All contractors and staff will receive site inductions that detail the location and presence of ecological values within the works area. These inductions will outline relevant protective measures and obligations during construction and mining activities.

#### 8.1.1.2 Native Vegetation Protection (EMo2 – EMo4)

The project will establish zones to protect areas of ecological value and prevent inadvertent damage from earthworks, vehicle parking, equipment storage, and stockpiling of soils and construction materials.

Two types of zones will be established:

- **No Go Zones (NGZs)**  
  Fencing will be erected:  
  - Along the edge of the Disturbance Footprint where it borders patches of native vegetation;  
  - Within two metres of native vegetation where the Disturbance Footprint is within 15 metres of such patches.

- **Tree Protection Zones (TPZs)**  
  TPZ fencing will be installed along the edge of a scattered tree if the Disturbance Footprint is within 15 metres of that tree.

TPZs will be established in accordance with *Australian Standard AS 4970-2009: Protection of Trees on Development Sites* (Standards Australia, 2009).

A register identifying the locations of NGZs and TPZs will be created and maintained. These zones will be clearly marked on all project plans and incorporated into the Biodiversity Management Plan (BMP). Regular inspections and maintenance of fencing will be conducted throughout construction and operations to ensure integrity.

---

#### 8.1.1.3 Marking of Access Tracks and Roads (EM23)

All access tracks and roads will be clearly marked to prevent the formation of secondary tracks and indirect impacts to native vegetation.

---

#### 8.1.1.4 Post-Clearing Auditing (EM22)

A post-clearing audit will be conducted at the end of each stage of development to reconcile the actual extent of native vegetation impacted against permitted losses.

---

### 8.1.2 Measures to Minimise Impacts on Retained Vegetation and Habitat

To reduce impacts on retained native vegetation and habitat, the following measures will be implemented. These include restricting works to the construction footprint, designating NGZs and TPZs (EM02, EM03, EM04), and adhering to sediment and pollution control best practices in line with Environment Protection Authority guidelines.

#### 8.1.2.1 Erosion and Sediment Control (EM24)

Appropriate sediment and erosion control measures will be implemented prior to ground-disturbance works and throughout construction and operations to protect retained vegetation and waterways.

Constructed landforms and sedimentation basins will be designed to minimise erosion and sedimentation off-target.

#### 8.1.2.2 Dust Management (EM16, EM25, EM27)

Dust control measures will be implemented, including:  
- Use of water sprays on unsealed roads and surfaces;  
- Application of dust suppression sprays and sprinklers;  
- Cessation of works during high-wind conditions;  
- Implementation of speed limits on haulage and internal access roads.

#### 8.1.2.3 Placement of Stockpiles, Machinery, and Roads (EM40)

Stockpiles, machinery, and infrastructure will be located as far as practicable from waterways, dams, and areas supporting native vegetation to minimise disturbance.

#### 8.1.2.4 Fire Management (EM33)

A bushfire risk assessment will be conducted to identify potential impacts and appropriate mitigation measures. Operational controls, including emergency response plans, will be developed and implemented and included in the BMP.

#### 8.1.2.5 Seed Collection (EM17)

Seed will be collected from specimens of nationally and state-significant flora species to preserve genetic diversity. While the focus is on threatened species, seed from non-threatened flora may also be collected. Collected seed will be used for rehabilitation and revegetation. Any surplus seed will be deposited with the RBGV Seed Bank.

#### 8.1.2.6 Chemical Spill Management (EM26)

All maintenance and refuelling activities will occur at safe distances with spill prevention measures in place to protect vegetation, habitat, and waterways, in accordance with relevant regulatory requirements.

Materials that may contaminate the environment (e.g., fuels, solvents) will be stored, handled, transported, and used in compliance with:  
- *Environmental Protection Act 2017*;  
- *Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2012*;  
- *Liquid Storage and Handling Guidelines (Publication 1698)*;  
- *Storage and Handling of Flammable and Combustible Liquids – AS 1940:2017*;  
- *WorkSafe Victoria* guidance.

#### 8.1.2.7 Mitigation of Impacts to Red Gum Swamp, Darragan Swamp, and Jallumba Marsh Flora and Fauna Reserve (EM28–EM32, EM42)

Although the project will not directly impact these wetlands, changes to the catchment may indirectly affect surface water flows. These impacts can be mitigated through the relocation of stockpiles, as the reserves are located immediately adjacent to the Disturbance Footprint.

Mitigation measures include:  
- Regulating supplementary water flows into Red Gum Swamp and Darragan Swamp to mimic natural variability using an alternate water source, in accordance with the *Wetland Watering Plan* (Water Technology, 2025) and regional seasonal patterns;  
- Bi-annual water quality testing in Red Gum Swamp and Darragan Swamp after supplementary flows begin to ensure no adverse effects on fauna or habitat;  
- Annual monitoring of habitat use by wetland-dependent fauna (e.g., frogs, waterbirds, turtles) at Red Gum Swamp and Darragan Swamp;  
- Annual monitoring of vegetation condition and extent at Red Gum Swamp, Darragan Swamp, and Jallumba Marsh;  
- During rehabilitation, final landforms and drainage will be designed to restore pre-disturbance catchment and drainage patterns.

### 8.1.3 Measures to Prevent Introduction of Weeds and Pathogens

Progressive mining cell development involves land stripping and stockpiling, which may introduce weeds and pathogens if not managed properly.

All soil is assumed to contain weed propagules; all water and damp soil is assumed to be infected with pathogens—unless tested otherwise.

#### 8.1.3.1 Hygiene Controls (EMo1, EMo8, EM41)

To limit the introduction and spread of weeds and pathogens, the following controls will be implemented:  
- A Biodiversity Management Plan (BMP) will be prepared, including measures to manage biosecurity threats (weeds, pathogens, pest animals);  
- All staff and contractors will be inducted on ecological values and protective measures;  
- Wash-down locations for machinery, vehicles, tools, and footwear will be established;  
- Gravel, crushed rock, or other non-soil substrates imported to site must be free from noxious weed seed;  
- Known areas of noxious weed infestations (listed under the *Catchment and Land Protection Act 1994* – CaLP Act) will be identified on site plans and contractor induction materials;  
- Driving will be restricted to surfaced or defined roads and tracks;  
- Avoid driving through highly infested areas, wetlands, or wet soil;  
- Any noxious weed infestation must be managed to prevent spread into adjacent land.

#### 8.1.3.2 Weed Management (EMo9)

A Weed Management Plan will be prepared, including:  
- Prior treatment of high-risk weeds before construction begins;  
- Engagement of experienced land managers familiar with native plant and weed identification, herbicide use, and mechanical removal techniques;  
- Ongoing monitoring and control of noxious weeds;  
- Regular inspections for signs of weed invasion, degradation, or erosion near work areas;  
- Prompt management of any new infestations of CaLP Act or National Environmental Significance (WoNS) species;  
- Ensuring all contractors are required to remove mud and plant material from vehicles and equipment before entering work sites.

### 8.1.4 Measures to Minimise Disturbance, Injury, or Death to Wildlife

The following measures will reduce the risk of fauna mortality and displacement during construction and operations.

#### 8.1.4.1 Pre-Clearance Surveys and Fauna Salvage (EMo5–EMo7)

A pre-clearance survey protocol and wildlife salvage plan will be developed in consultation with DEECA and included in the BMP. A qualified zoologist will conduct pre-clearance searches in any identified fauna habitats.

A suitably qualified wildlife handler (‘wildlife spotter’), holding current authorisation under the *Wildlife Act 1975*, will be engaged to salvage and relocate wildlife encountered during site clearance.

An **Injured Wildlife Protocol** will be developed and implemented in consultation with DEECA and local wildlife carers, outlining steps for handling injured animals and providing contact details for care services.

---

#### 8.1.4.2 Timing of Habitat Removal (EM14)

Habitat removal (especially hollow-bearing trees) will be staged to allow fauna to disperse away from the construction area.

Removal will occur between **February and September** to avoid breeding seasons for most species.  
No removal will occur between **October and January inclusive**, unless justified under protocols in the BMP.

---

#### 8.1.4.3 Replacement Hollows (EM12)

To compensate for the removal of hollow-bearing trees, **nest boxes** will be installed in adjacent retained habitats. The number of nest boxes will exceed the number of trees removed.

Suitable habitat (e.g., logs, trunks, stags) removed within the Disturbance Footprint will be repurposed and relocated outside the footprint.

Nest boxes (artificial wood, 3D-printed, or carved logs) will be secured at least **2 metres above ground**, in shaded areas, near food and water sources, and out of prevailing wet-weather winds.

Any existing hollows in trees to be impacted will be reinstalled in retained vegetation outside the development footprint.

Investigations into viable hollow replacements will be conducted during post-approval stages, with site-specific effectiveness for target species considered in the BMP.

##### Artificial Nest Boxes

Traditional nest boxes are often under-utilised (McNabb & Greenwood, 2011) and may be used by non-native fauna (Lindenmayer et al., 2017) or disintegrate within a few years.  
Recent innovations include:

##### 3D-Printed Nest Boxes

Trials in the City of Knox have shown 3D-printed boxes can mimic natural nesting sites of Powerful Owls. Made from organic materials (e.g., hemp concrete, clay, fungus), these lightweight boxes can be easily attached to trees.

##### Installation of Carved Hollows and/or Logs

Carved hollows into live trees provide better thermal and physical properties than artificial boxes (Terry et al., 2021). They offer stable microclimates, buffering fauna from extreme weather.  
Carved hollows and logs provide long-term, sustainable, ecologically robust habitat, increasing breeding success for hollow-dependent species.

#### 8.1.4.4 Fauna Escape Mechanisms (EM13)

Trenching works will incorporate fauna escape measures in accordance with the *APGA Code of Environmental Practice: Onshore Pipelines* (AGPA, 2022), including:  
- Minimising the length and duration open trenches are exposed;  
- Constructing trench plugs (unexcavated sections) with slopes less than 45 degrees to allow exit ramps for fauna;  
- Installing exit ramps at the ends of cable trenches where practicable;  
- Providing ‘ladders’ (e.g., ropes, planks, floats) to enable fauna to exit;  
- Daily inspections of open trenches by certified personnel to remove trapped animals;  
- Checking for fauna prior to backfilling;  
- Erecting barriers where practicable to discourage access;  
- Installing signage along access routes through conservation areas to raise awareness of wildlife crossings;  
- Developing a protocol for locating and rescuing trapped fauna.

---

#### 8.1.4.5 Wildlife-Friendly Fence Design (EM15)

To reduce entanglement and damage, fencing will be designed to be visible and permeable to wildlife, where possible. Exceptions will be made only where hazardous (e.g., chain mesh fencing >1.8 m high to deter kangaroos).

- **Permanent NGZ fencing** will use post-and-wire or chain mesh fencing without barbed wire.  
- A white wire strand will be added at the top to improve visibility.  
- Design choice (post and wire vs. chain mesh) will be site-specific and determined prior to construction.

- **Temporary NGZs** will use a single wire strand with bunting flags instead of orange mesh para-webbing to reduce entanglement risk.

---

#### 8.1.4.6 Fauna-Sensitive Lighting Design (EM11)

In areas near sensitive fauna, lighting will be designed to minimise disturbance, in accordance with the *National Light Pollution Guidelines for Wildlife* (DCCEEW, 2023a):  
- Use light technology that limits light spill beyond the works area (e.g., shielded, ground-mounted lights);  
- Use red or amber filters to reduce attraction to insects;  
- Facilities near sensitive habitats will use low-intensity, long-wavelength lighting (e.g., red/orange) where safety permits;  
- Low-pressure sodium lamps and amber LEDs emit low blue/UV light, which is less disruptive to wildlife.

---

#### 8.1.4.7 Traffic Management (EM16)

To reduce wildlife-vehicle interactions:  
- Speed limits will be enforced on haulage and internal access roads;  
- Agreed speed limits will be maintained as per occupational health and safety standards;  
- Wildlife warning signage will be installed and maintained;  
- Vehicle-wildlife interactions will be recorded to inform review of management practices;  
- Canopy bridges will be installed over access and haulage roads to allow arboreal fauna to cross safely.

---

#### 8.1.4.8 Noise and Vibration Abatement (EM36–EM39)

Despite proximity to ecologically sensitive areas (e.g., Jallumba Marsh, Darragan Swamp), noise and vibration impacts will be mitigated through:  
- A **Fauna Management Plan** identifying key breeding areas (e.g., Growling Grass Frog) and associated sensitive times to limit high-impact activities;  
- Use of noise baffles or mufflers on high-noise equipment;  
- Limiting high-impact noise events to daylight hours and outside key breeding periods;  
- Traffic noise levels will not exceed those specified in the *VicRoads Traffic Noise Reduction Policy*.

---

#### 8.1.4.9 Maintenance and Enhancement of Fauna Connectivity and Habitat (EM15, EM18–EM21)

As vegetation is progressively removed, opportunities exist to rehabilitate and revegetate disturbed areas with local indigenous species.

Measures to maintain connectivity include:  
- Establishing fauna-friendly fencing to facilitate movement;  
- Commencing revegetation outside the mine footprint as soon as practicable to reduce habitat delay;  
- Prioritising the creation of a habitat corridor adjacent to the mine footprint to compensate for the loss of the existing corridor along Jallumba-Mockinya Road;  
- Retaining timber (trunks and logs) from cleared vegetation for reuse in retained or rehabilitated areas;  
- Retaining and relocating rocks to provide habitat for reptiles.

Although the mine layout is largely defined by resource extent, ongoing alignment refinements offer opportunities to avoid impacts to native trees and fauna habitats at a local scale. A detailed assessment of large trees, scattered trees, and associated fauna habitats was conducted to support retention where possible.

---

### 8.1.5 Management Plans

Effective implementation of mitigation measures depends on comprehensive management plans that define responsibilities, timelines, performance objectives, and monitoring protocols.

As part of ongoing project planning, detailed contingency and mitigation measures will be included in the **Project Risk Management Plan**.

The **Biodiversity Management Plan (BMP)** will be a core component, covering biodiversity monitoring, management, and performance during construction, operations, and rehabilitation. The BMP will include sub-plans for:  
- Flora and Fauna Management  
- Weed and Pest Animal Management  
- Threatened Species Protection  
- Pre-Clearance Protocol and Fauna Salvage  
- Wetland Watering Plan  
- Rehabilitation and Revegetation Plan  
- Groundwater Monitoring Plan  
- Offset Management Plan (Section 9.2)

These plans will be submitted to **ERR, DEECA, and DTP** for review and approval. They may be updated as required during the approval process.

The BMP will:  
- Set out accountabilities and systems for managing environmental effects;  
- Define approaches to evaluating and reporting environmental outcomes;  
- Include an ongoing biodiversity monitoring program prior to construction and during operations.

Monitoring will focus on retained ecological values and assess:  
- The quality and extent of retained vegetation against baseline data;  
- Thresholds of change to trigger management responses (sensitive enough to prevent irreversible damage);  
- Contingency measures for adverse residual impacts to flora and fauna.
