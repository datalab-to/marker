### 9.2.3 Growling Grass Frog

#### 9.2.3.1 Proposed Impacts

Between 2–4 Growling Grass Frog were heard calling at Darragan Swamp during the 2022 breeding season. No Growling Grass Frog were recorded at any of the other seven wetlands investigated during the survey effort, and based on the location of existing records, it is considered highly unlikely that any other resident populations of the species are present or rely on habitat within the Project Area, or are able to access additional habitat within the Project Area if it is not already present.

Indirect impacts such as changes to water quality, quantity, and hydrology as a result of the proposed project may negatively impact this population of Growling Grass Frog.

Darragan Swamp is an ephemeral wetland that fills regularly and draws down relatively quickly, with water sourced from direct catchment runoff without any connection to river floodplain flows (Water Technology 2025). It is located outside the proposed development footprint, approximately 6 kilometres north-east of Jallumba Marsh.

Modelling indicates that Darragan Swamp routinely drops from full or near full to near empty or dry within 1–2 years. The modelling also predicts that Darragan Swamp experiences more regular dry periods, with these periods being quite short and occurring on average once every 5 years (Water Technology 2025). Darragan Swamp has a clearly defined outlet channel heading north from the northern end of the wetland. The depth of the wetland is only 0.9 m, and results show that the wetland storage reaches capacity quite often and spills to downstream receptors at least every one to two years on average, with water levels in the swamp primarily driven by inflow during the wet season in each year, rather than a result of a prolonged multi-year wet period (Water Technology 2025).

An analysis of potential impacts to Darragan Swamp found that the wetland will be impacted from early in the mine life, with activities scheduled to start from the northern areas, resulting in a reduction in catchment area and reduced inflows (Water Technology 2025). However, the reduction in inflows is relatively minor and does not significantly alter the hydraulic regime of the wetland, given that Darragan fills regularly and draws down quickly. The reduced inflows are unlikely to change the ecological character of the wetland.

However, the installation of supplementary pipeline infrastructure is proposed to enable delivery of supplementary water to mitigate any potential hydrological impact to the wetland and ensure that the current hydrological regime is maintained to ensure the suitability of Darragan Swamp as a breeding and foraging habitat for Growling Grass Frog is not compromised.

#### 9.2.3.2 Proposed Offsets

Provided the Wetland Watering Plan (Water Technology 2025) is implemented to ensure that the suitability of Darragan Swamp as a breeding site for Growling Grass Frog is maintained, and the inundation regime that would have occurred pre-mining is maintained in a post-mining scenario (with reference to seasonal conditions within the region), no impacts to Growling Grass Frog are assumed, and no offsets are proposed.

### 9.2.6 Seasonal Herbaceous Wetlands (Freshwater) of the Temperate Lowland Plains

#### 9.2.6.1 Proposed Impacts

The nationally significant community Seasonal Herbaceous Wetlands (Freshwater) of the Temperate Lowland Plains (SHW community) is present in one large patch totalling 22.434 hectares located within Jallumba Marsh Flora Reserve.

Based on the current Disturbance Footprint, a total of **0.05 hectares** of the SHW community will be impacted as a result of an internal road proposed to be constructed adjacent to Jallumba Marsh Flora Reserve (Figure 2a-vii). The next iteration of the Disturbance Footprint will be amended to avoid impacts to the SHW community.

Indirect impacts such as changes to water quality, quantity, and hydrology as a result of the proposed project may negatively impact the community and result in a significant impact.

An assessment of potential indirect impacts to Jallumba Marsh Flora Reserve found that a water reduction will occur, although it is modelled to correlate with wetter years only (Water Technology 2025). However, this impact can be minimised through the relocation of stockpiles to restore runoff from the unaffected catchment that feeds Jallumba Marsh Flora Reserve from this direction, and as such, no supplementary watering would be required to maintain existing values (i.e., SHW community).

Based on the results of the hydrological analysis undertaken by Water Technology (2025), no indirect impacts are assumed to occur to the SHW community.

#### 9.2.6.2 Proposed Offsets

The existing proposed impact of 0.05 hectares is not considered to be a significant impact to the ecological community, and no offsets are proposed.

However, it is recommended that Iluka review the internal road alignment located directly adjacent to the SHW community to ensure no impact occurs. A buffer should be implemented between the Disturbance Footprint to mitigate against indirect impacts during construction and operation. Further review of potential impacts and offsets associated with the SHW community will be undertaken as preparation of the Wetland Watering Plan (Water Technology 2025) progresses and as further iterations to the Disturbance Footprint occur.

### 9.2.7 Migratory Species

Based on the significant impact guidelines for migratory shorebirds (DoEE 2017), an action is likely to have a significant impact on important habitat for a migratory species if there is a real chance or possibility that it will:

- substantially modify (including by fragmenting, altering fire regimes, altering nutrient cycles, or altering hydrological cycles), destroy or isolate an area of important habitat for a migratory species;
- result in an invasive species that is harmful to the migratory species becoming established in an area of important habitat for the migratory species; or,
- seriously disrupt the lifecycle (breeding, feeding, migration, or resting behaviour) of an ecologically significant proportion of the population of a migratory species.

The Project Area is not considered to be classified as **internationally** important habitat for migratory shorebirds as defined in DoEE (2017), as wetland habitat within the Assessment Area does not regularly support:

- 1% of the individuals of a population of one species or subspecies of waterbird; or  
- a total abundance of at least 20,000 waterbirds.

The Project Area is also not considered to be classified as **nationally** important habitat for migratory shorebirds as defined in DoEE (2017), as wetland habitat within the Assessment Area does not regularly support:

- 0.1% of the flyway population of a single species of migratory shorebird; or  
- 2,000 migratory shorebirds; or  
- 15 migratory shorebird species.
